The elephant familiar to most safari-goers is, technically speaking, the African bush elephant, a distinct species from the smaller African forest elephant. It is a complex, social, intelligent and resilient animal, a keystone species in its ecosystem. Elephants help shape the land, create habitats for other species, and are vital to the health of the savanna and forests. Their presence keeps the balance of nature, and their story is one of survival, connection and hope.

Habitat:Woodland and savanna
Scientific name:Loxodonta africana
Adult weight:2,000 – 6,300 kg
IUCN status:Vulnerable
